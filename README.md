# XS-BNF
XS-BNF (Xu Shen–Backus-Naur Form) fuses the classical Six Writings of Shuowen Jiezi with Backus-Naur Form, giving machines an explicit, recursive grammar for Chinese structure. Includes SWPC-BNF (character level) and SWWC-BNF (word level), validated across multiple LLM platforms through cross-model experiments and human-verified annotation.
# XS-BNF (许慎–巴科斯–诺尔范式 / Xu Shen–Backus-Naur Form)

A recursive, formally-verifiable grammar for Chinese structure — from strokes to sentences — designed to give large language models an explicit, auditable representation of Chinese, rather than a purely statistical one.**

XS-BNF unites the classical Six Writings (六书) principles first systematized in Xu Shen's <Shuowen Jiezi> (说文解字, 100 CE) with the Backus–Naur Form (BNF) used to specify programming-language grammars. The result is a small set of composable, recursive production rules that describe how Chinese characters and words are built — and that can be parsed, validated, compared, and generated by machines, not just read by humans.

---

## Why

Mainstream LLMs process Chinese as sequences of (sub-)word tokens produced by BPE/Unigram/SentencePiece. This works remarkably well in practice, but it leaves the internal compositional structure of Chinese characters and words — which part is the semantic head, which part is a modifier, which compounds are transparent and which have drifted into fixed, non-compositional meanings — entirely implicit. XS-BNF makes that structure explicit and machine-checkable.

## The Four-Layer Architecture

XS-BNF is designed as a four-level formal hierarchy, mirroring the levels at which human readers process Chinese:

| Layer | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|
| MORF-BNF | Letter/morpheme level (cross-script extension) | Research proposal, not yet implemented |
| SWPC-BNF | Character level (六书 / component structure) | ✅ Implemented and validated (see below) |
| SWWC-BNF | Word level (compound-word structure) | ✅ Implemented and validated (see below) |
| SWSC-BNF | Sentence level (syntax / information focus) | Research proposal, not yet implemented |

Only SWPC-BNF and SWWC-BNF are implemented and empirically tested in this repository at present. MORF-BNF and SWSC-BNF are documented as a future research direction, not as delivered components — please do not cite them as validated.

---

SWPC-BNF — Character Level

Represents each Hanzi as an explicit structural expression rather than an atomic symbol, using a small set of construction operators (e.g. `LR`, `TB`, `LMR`, `TMB`), a pair of orientation operators (`/`, `‡`) marking radical placement, and glyph-variant operators (`↑`, `↕`, `↓`) linking a component to its simplified/traditional or historically-related forms — e.g. `贝(Mj)` / `貝(Mj↑)`.

- Manually verified database: **3,866 characters**, 13 IDS-aligned structural categories
- Recursive reuse reduces leaf components by ~56%, representation depth by ~47%, and matching cost by up to ~76% versus full expansion
- Published: *SWPC-BNF: Recursive Structure-Constrained Representation of Chinese Characters*, presented at ICPR 2026 (Lyon, France)

SWWC-BNF — Word Level

Extends the same design philosophy to Chinese compound words: 18 operators across four layers (core structural — coordinate/modifier-head/verb-object/subject-predicate/prefix/suffix/reduplication and their semantic sub-types; complement — resultative/directional; pragmatic — tentative/repetitive/disjunctive; and a meta layer — lexicalization marking with reliability tagging, and template-failure/alternative handling).

- Cross-platform teaching–transfer experiments across multiple LLMs, probing structural reasoning (not just fluency) on trap cases such as same-morpheme reversals (计算/算计), OOV generation (小木师), and semantic-head migration in lexicalized compounds (雪白/白雪)
- A multi-platform annotation pipeline (LLM → LLM → LLM arbitration → human verification) has produced a **human-verified, 3,000-word gold-standard annotation set** (≈75% cumulative frequency coverage of modern Mandarin), iteratively refining the operator set from v1 through v2.2
- Two papers reporting this work are under review at IALP 2026

## Design Principles

1. Recursive production over enumeration** — a small operator set generates unbounded valid structures, rather than listing them.
2. Explicit semantic head** — orientation operators (`/`, `‡`) remove head-position ambiguity from every binary construction, at both the character and word level.
3. Structure–pragmatics separation** — core lexical-grammatical relations are kept distinct from operators marking speaker attitude, aspect, or register.
4. Ambiguity tolerance, not false precision** — where a compound genuinely admits multiple valid parses, the framework records that rather than forcing a single answer.
5. Honesty about coverage** — a dedicated `UNF`/`ALT` mechanism lets the system flag "this template does not actually fit" instead of silently producing a well-formed but non-existent word.

## Current Limitations

- Validated primarily via in-context (few-shot) LLM prompting; **not yet** tested as a structured tokenizer, embedding initializer, or auxiliary training loss (this is the intended next step — see below).
- Downstream task gains (e.g. machine translation, OOV segmentation) have not yet been measured.
- Operator judgments to date rely on the research team's own verification against primary sources (e.g. *Shuowen Jiezi* and its Qing-dynasty commentaries) rather than review by professional Chinese historical linguists; such review is a planned next step, not yet completed.

## Roadmap

- [x] SWPC-BNF character-level formalism + 3,866-character database
- [x] SWWC-BNF word-level formalism (v1 → v2.2) + 3,000-word gold-standard annotation
- [x] CJK-SQL prototype (PostgreSQL, recursive JSON structure search)
- [ ] Scale word-level annotation beyond 3,000 words via a rule-based generation + human-audit pipeline (rather than word-by-word manual annotation)
- [ ] Structured knowledge graph integrating character- and word-level data
- [ ] Pilot experiment: SWWC-BNF-informed embedding initialization vs. standard tokenizer initialization on a small model
- [ ] SWSC-BNF (sentence level) and MORF-BNF (cross-script level) — research proposals only at this stage

## A Note on AI Assistance

This project's word-level annotations were produced through a multi-LLM pipeline (independent annotation → cross-model arbitration → human verification) rather than by a single model or a single annotator; the specific platforms and division of labor are documented in `experiments/`. Manuscript and documentation preparation for associated publications received LLM writing assistance (see individual paper acknowledgments); all theoretical contributions, experimental design, and final judgments remain the responsibility of the human research team.
